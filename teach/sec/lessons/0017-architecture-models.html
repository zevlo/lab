Architecture Models — Home Turf, Exam Words

One win today: name the right architecture model for any scenario — and say exactly where the responsibility line sits: who patches what. Compressed track: you run these systems daily. The lesson's job is attaching exam vocabulary to what your hands already know.

Why this matters to your mission

DoD lives in this objective: defense contractors share community clouds built around common clearance requirements, the department runs hybrid architectures, and industrial control systems are nation-state targets — grid attacks start at the SCADA layer. In a DoD 8140 role, "who patches what" is not philosophy; it is the first audit question after any cloud incident.

Practice — 8 questions, timed

Four marked MULTI-HOP combine frameworks from earlier lessons. One marked BEST ANSWER has two defensible options — pick the better. Question 1 repairs the week-2 review's miss. Budget: 12 minutes for 12 questions — the exam's pace. Stopwatch: 0:00

QUESTION 1

A standard user account is caught reading another department's mailboxes. The account's own permissions never changed. Which escalation direction is this?

Horizontal — same rights, new victims. The rule from the week-2 miss: the axis is what changed for the attacker, never what the data is worth. Rights grew beyond the account's level = vertical. Same rights reaching other same-tier accounts = horizontal. (Lesson 0014; 0016 Q1 repair.)

QUESTION 2 MULTI-HOP

A critical operating system flaw affects your cloud virtual machines, and the machine images are yours to build. Under which model is patching that OS your job?

Infrastructure as a service. Your images, your OS, your patch — the ladder's bottom rung. SaaS would make it the vendor's patch; serverless would mean there is no OS for you to see. (Responsibility ladder plus Lesson 0015's patching.)

QUESTION 3

Thousands of isolated application instances share one host kernel — no guest operating systems anywhere. Which technology?

Containerization. Shared kernel, no guest OS — that is the density win. Virtual machines each carry a full guest OS: stronger isolation, far less density.

QUESTION 4 MULTI-HOP

A water utility's programmable logic controllers can never be patched. Which mitigation does the exam pair with exactly this situation?

Isolation from other networks. Unpatchable industrial controllers are the textbook isolation case — ICS availability matters more than anything, and the boundary is the only defense. (Lessons 0015, this lesson's ICS/SCADA row.)

QUESTION 5 BEST ANSWER

The platform adds servers before the sale and removes them after — capacity grows and shrinks with demand. Which property is best named?

Elasticity — grow and shrink on demand. Scalability is the defensible near miss: it says capacity can grow. The "removes them after" is the tell — bidirectional is elasticity, one-directional is scalability.

QUESTION 6

A hypervisor runs directly on the server's hardware, with no host operating system beneath it. Which type?

Type 1. Bare-metal: guests sit on the hypervisor, the hypervisor sits on hardware. Type 2 runs as an application inside a host OS — one more layer to attack. There is no type 3.

QUESTION 7 MULTI-HOP

Your event-triggered functions run on the provider's runtime — you have never seen the server and never patched one. Who patches the runtime, and which model is this?

The provider patches; serverless. The responsibility ladder's top rung — you bring code, the provider brings everything else. (This lesson's ladder plus Lesson 0015's patching boundary.)

QUESTION 8 MULTI-HOP

Several defense contractors share a cloud operated for their common clearance and compliance requirements — open to no one else. Which deployment model?

Community cloud. Organizations with common concerns sharing dedicated infrastructure — compliance as the membership card. Hybrid would mix private and public for one organization. (This lesson, plus the DoD mission box.)
