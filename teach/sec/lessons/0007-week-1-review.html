Part 1 — Mixed quiz: 20 questions, timed

Budget: 20 minutes for 20 questions — the exam's pace. The stopwatch starts at your first answer and stops at your last. Answer from memory; the explanation names the lesson only after you answer. Stopwatch: 0:00

QUESTION 1

A guard dog patrols the loading dock. Which control category and type is this?

Physical; deterrent. Something you can touch, working on the attacker's motivation rather than mechanics. (Lessons 0001, 0002.)

QUESTION 2

An attacker alters a wire transfer amount while it is in transit. Which goal is harmed?

Integrity. Data altered in transit is the canonical integrity harm; hashing and signatures defend against it. (Lessons 0002, 0004.)

QUESTION 3

A client asks the certificate authority's responder in real time whether a certificate is still good. Which mechanism?

Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP). Real-time validity queries; stapling lets the server present the proof itself. (Lesson 0005.)

QUESTION 4

A change fails in production, and the previous state is restored using the procedure that shipped with it. Which control type just fired?

Corrective. It repaired the damage after the failure — corrective's exact job. (Lessons 0001, 0006.)

QUESTION 5

Encrypting a message before sending it provides which protection, using which key?

Confidentiality with the recipient's public key. Public encrypts, only the matching private key decrypts. Private signs, public verifies — that is the signature side. (Lesson 0004.)

QUESTION 6

The security team reviews firewall logs on the first Monday of every month. Which control category?

Operational. People running a recurring process — log review is the classic operational example, and a detective type. (Lesson 0001.)

QUESTION 7

Two users have the same password, but their stored hashes differ. What made that happen?

A salt. A random value mixed in before hashing, so identical inputs fingerprint differently. (Lesson 0004.)

QUESTION 8

Which principle states that network location grants nothing and every request must prove itself?

Never trust, always verify. The zero-trust summary. "Trust the internal zone" is the implicit-trust anti-pattern zero trust exists to kill. (Lesson 0003.)

QUESTION 9

A self-signed certificate works fine between two lab servers but fails in a browser. Why?

No trusted root backs it. The chain of trust has one link — itself. Browsers trust only chains that climb to an installed root. (Lesson 0005.)

QUESTION 10

Verification starts with the presented certificate and climbs through an intermediate to an anchor the system already trusts. What is being walked?

A chain of trust. Leaf, intermediate, root — each level's signature checked by the level above. (Lesson 0005.)

QUESTION 11

A policy states no change reaches production without approval, and violations go to management review. Which category and type?

Managerial; directive. Instructions enforced through management; steers behavior rather than blocking mechanics. (Lessons 0001, 0006.)

QUESTION 12

A planted file named "salaries.xlsx" trips an alert the moment someone opens it. What is it called?

A honeyfile. A decoy document — the file-level sibling of a decoy system (honeypot) or decoy network (honeynet). (Lesson 0003.)

QUESTION 13

A retailer stores meaningless tokens in place of card numbers; the real mapping sits in a separate vault. Which technique?

Tokenization. Substitute a fake value, keep the mapping elsewhere. Masking would only hide part of the real value. (Lesson 0005.)

QUESTION 14

A switch replacement is implemented Sunday at 02:00 because usage is lowest then. What is that time slot called?

A maintenance window. A pre-agreed, low-usage slot — the availability-protecting step of the change process. (Lesson 0006.)

QUESTION 15

In the change process, which activity sits between approval and scheduling?

Testing. Approved changes are proven in a test environment or sandbox before a window is booked. (Lesson 0006.)

QUESTION 16

A finance role can open the payroll folder but not the engineering plans. Which element of AAA is this?

Authorization. What an authenticated identity is allowed to do — permissions are authorization's territory. (Lesson 0002.)

QUESTION 17

An encrypted VPN replaces a private circuit the organization could not afford. Which control type?

Compensating. A substitute used when the ideal control is not possible — the textbook example. (Lesson 0001.)

QUESTION 18

Who does a change advisory board bring to the table?

The affected stakeholders. Databases, network, application owners, users — the people who own the consequences judge the risk. (Lesson 0006.)

QUESTION 19

An internet-facing server is being actively exploited, and a vendor patch exists. Which action is best?

Patch now under the emergency process, documenting after. The emergency path exists for active exploitation — expedited approval now, complete record after. Waiting leaves the attack running; rebuilding destroys evidence. (Lesson 0006.)

QUESTION 20

Tamper with an old block in a distributed ledger and every later block's recorded hash stops matching. What property does this demonstrate?

Tamper-evident. The tamper is not prevented — it is detected, because the break cascades forward through chained hashes. (Lessons 0004, 0005.)

Part 2 — Performance-based exercise: classify the control

This mimics the exam's performance-based questions (PBQs): you act instead of choosing. Not timed — on exam day, PBQs appear first, and you may skip them, bank the multiple choice, and return. Assign each scenario its control type.

ITEM 1

A fence surrounds the parking lot.

Preventive. A physical barrier stops the attempt from succeeding — it does not merely discourage it. (Lesson 0001.)

ITEM 2

A sign warns: "Cameras in use — premises monitored."

Deterrent. The sign works on motivation before any attempt. (If the camera itself catches the act, that part is detective — but the sign is deterrence.) (Lesson 0001.)

ITEM 3

An intrusion detection system raises an alert during a port scan.

Detective. It discovers and reports; it does not block. Its sibling the IPS blocks — preventive. (Lesson 0001.)

ITEM 4

After ransomware encrypts a file server, yesterday's backup is restored.

Corrective. Repair after the incident — restoration is corrective's signature move. (Lesson 0001.)

ITEM 5

Patching the vulnerable IoT camera fleet proves impossible, so the cameras are isolated on their own network segment.

Compensating. The ideal control (patching) is impossible, so a substitute reduces the risk instead. (Lessons 0001, 0003.)

ITEM 6

The acceptable use policy tells employees how company systems may be used.

Directive. It directs behavior — compliance steers rather than blocks, deters, detects, or repairs. (Lesson 0001.)
