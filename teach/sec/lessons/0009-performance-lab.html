Performance Lab: Five Stations

One win today: work through the exam's five performance-based question types by doing them — dragging, building, reading, placing, and calculating. No option buttons wearing a costume. Each station checks your work and explains every miss.

Why this matters to your mission

A performance-based question (PBQ) makes you act — drag items, configure a rule, read a log — instead of picking a letter. Several appear at the start of your 90 minutes, and they are skippable: skim, move to the multiple choice, and return with time banked. Lesson 0007 gave you one stylized exercise; today you get the real shapes. These five tasks are also the daily work of a DoD 8140 security role: writing rules, reading logs, placing devices, and ranking risk.

How every station works

  • Drag a card into a box — or click the card, then click the box.

  • Click log rows to mark them. Click again to unmark.
  • Type short answers where asked. Numbers may be typed with or without commas.
  • Check grades the station and explains. Reset returns the cards. Nothing is timed.

Station 2 — Firewall rules and ACLs

An access control list (ACL) is an ordered list of ALLOW and DENY rules on a router or firewall. Three facts decide everything:

  • The list is read top to bottom. The first matching rule wins, and the rest are never checked.
  • A default deny (deny all) at the bottom is the safe pattern: anything you did not permit is dropped.
  • This firewall is stateful: replies to allowed connections return automatically. You never write a "back" rule.

Station 4 — Place the devices on the diagram

One paragraph of theory. A demilitarized zone (DMZ) is a buffer network between the internet and the internal network, holding servers that must be public. If a DMZ server falls, the internal network is still behind its own firewall — defense in depth. An intrusion detection system (IDS) watches a copy of the traffic and alerts; it does not block. A virtual private network (VPN) concentrator terminates encrypted remote connections. A SIEM collects logs from every device into one place.

Station 5 — Vulnerability triage and risk math

Two skills. First, ranking scan findings. Second, the four risk numbers.

NumberFull nameFormula
SLESingle-loss expectancy — the cost of one bad eventAsset value × Exposure factor
AROAnnualized rate of occurrence — how often per yearGiven (a frequency)
ALEAnnualized loss expectancy — expected loss per yearSLE × ARO

For vulnerability findings, priority = severity × exposure × active exploitation. A CVSS score alone does not decide. CVSS bands: 9.0–10.0 critical, 7.0–8.9 high, 4.0–6.9 medium, 0.1–3.9 low.

Part B — Calculate the risk

The warehouse and its contents are worth $2,400,000 (asset value). A serious flood would destroy about 25 percent of that (exposure factor). Floods of that size hit about once every 10 years. A levee upgrade would cost $15,000 per year and would halve the chance of flooding — to once every 20 years.
