Security Controls

By the end you can do one thing: take any control the exam describes and name its category and its type. That single skill answers a whole family of exam questions — and it is the daily work of one of the DoD 8140 roles this exam unlocks.

Why this matters to your mission

The US Department of Defense (DoD) 8140 directive lists a work role called security control assessor. That person's entire job is looking at an organization's controls and judging whether they work. CompTIA built objective 1.1 as the vocabulary sheet for that job. Every later domain — threats, architecture, operations — describes controls. This lesson is the foundation.

The idea in three sentences

A security control is anything an organization puts in place to reduce risk: a device, a process, a document, or a physical barrier. Every control answers two questions. What kind of thing is it? — that is its category. What job does it do? — that is its type.

Full definitions live in the glossary; acronyms used here are in the acronym sheet.

Step 1 — The four categories: what kind of thing is it?

CategoryTestExamples
ManagerialIt is a decision or instruction, written down and enforced by management.Policies, risk assessments, disaster recovery plans
OperationalA person runs a process, day to day.Log reviews, incident response, security training
TechnicalA machine does the work.Firewalls, encryption, intrusion detection systems (IDS)
PhysicalYou can touch it.Fences, locks, cameras (CCTV), guard dogs

Watch for the trap: operational means a person performs the process — not "anything that operates." A firewall is technical even though it operates.

Step 2 — The six types: what job does it do?

TypePlain meaning
PreventiveStops the attack from succeeding.
DeterrentDiscourages the attempt. Works on motivation, not mechanics.
DetectiveDiscovers the attack, during or after.
CorrectiveRepairs the damage after the attack.
CompensatingA substitute, used when the ideal control is not possible.
DirectiveTells people what to do. Steers behavior instead of blocking it.

Classify in five seconds

  1. Who or what enforces it? Machine → technical. Document or decision → managerial. Person doing a process → operational. Something you touch → physical.
  2. When does it act, and on what? Stops the act → preventive. Discourages trying → deterrent. Reveals the act → detective. Repairs after → corrective. Substitutes for the impossible ideal → compensating. Instructs behavior → directive.
  3. Two answers both fit? The scenario gives a clue. Choose the fit the question emphasizes. CompTIA cares about best answer, not the only answer.

Practice — 8 questions, instant feedback

From Lesson 0002 onward, each lesson opens with 3 recall questions from earlier lessons. Today is Lesson 1, so these questions are all new.

QUESTION 1

A chain-link fence surrounds the parking lot. Which category is this control?

Physical. You can touch a fence. Its job here — stopping vehicles from reaching the building — makes it preventive.

QUESTION 2

A posted sign says trespassers will be prosecuted. Which type is this control?

Deterrent. The sign stops nothing mechanically. It works on motivation — it discourages the attempt.

QUESTION 3

An intrusion prevention system drops attack packets in real time. Which category and type?

Technical; preventive. A machine does the work, and it stops the attack. The IDS cousin only alerts — that would be detective.

QUESTION 4

Each week an analyst reviews firewall logs by hand. Which category and type?

Operational; detective. A person runs a process, and the process looks for what already happened.

QUESTION 5

After ransomware encrypts a file server, the team restores files from backup. Which type?

Corrective. The action happens after the damage, and its job is repair.

QUESTION 6

A document states the rules for using company email. Which category and type?

Managerial; directive. A policy is a document that directs behavior. If it lists punishments, it also acts as a deterrent — the scenario's emphasis decides.

QUESTION 7

A leased private circuit is too expensive, so the team encrypts internet traffic with a VPN. Which type?

Compensating. The ideal control was impossible, so a substitute was chosen. This is the exam's classic VPN example.

QUESTION 8

A guard dog patrols the yard at night. Which category and type?

Physical; deterrent. The dog is physical — touch with care — and the job is discouragement: most attackers pick another yard.
