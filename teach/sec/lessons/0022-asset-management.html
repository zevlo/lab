Inventory to Incinerator

One win today: account for every asset from procurement to destruction — and pick the sanitization level that fits where the media goes next. The exam's 4.2 questions live in one ladder: erase, clear, purge, destroy. Choose the rung by the data's classification and the media's destination.

Why this matters to your mission

Defense property accountability is law, not habit: every asset has a custodian, every disposition is documented, and classified media leaves custody one way — destroyed, with a certificate to prove it. The 4.2 exam questions are that discipline in scenario form. They are also where your DevOps inventory instincts get their exam names.

Warm-up — typed recall, no choices

W1 is the SAE reframe from Lesson 0021's miss. The anchor: SAE = simultaneous authentication of equals — the WPA3 handshake whose capture is worthless offline. Capture is not crack. Relay and on-path attacks are answered by mutual authentication — certificates, EAP — never by handshake hardness.

Part 3 — The sanitization ladder

RungWhat happensMedia afterFits
EraseDelete, format — pointers removed, data remains.Usable, data recoverable by anyone with tools.Nothing sensitive. Ever.
ClearOverwrite every sector with zeros or a pattern.Usable; recovery needs a laboratory.Reuse inside the organization at the same sensitivity.
PurgeDegauss (magnetic media) or crypto-erase (destroy the key).Degauss kills the drive; crypto-erase leaves it blank and alive.Media leaving your custody with anything sensitive on it.
DestroyShred, incinerate, pulverize.Not media anymore — particles.Classified and highest-sensitivity data. The only answer with no "effectively" in front of it.

The two purge tools, and the media trap

ToolHow it worksThe trap
DegausserA magnetic field so strong it erases everything — data, servo tracks, firmware.Magnetic media only — hard disks and tape. Useless on SSDs. And the drive is dead afterward: purged by execution.
Crypto-eraseDestroy the encryption key; every sector becomes noise at once.Requires the data was already encrypted — it is instant only if it was encrypted from the start. Lesson 0021's full-device encryption, meeting its destiny.

The SSD exception

Overwriting assumes you can reach every sector. Solid-state drives wear-level — they silently spread writes and retire sectors, so an overwrite can miss data the controller is hiding. For SSDs the answer is crypto-erase or destroy. The degausser, meanwhile, does nothing at all: no magnetic surface.

When disposal is outsourced, the vendor returns a certificate of destruction (or of sanitization) — the signed record that closes the audit chain. It is a managerial control: proof, not protection. E-waste rules ride along: disposal must be both secure and compliant.

Practice — 8 questions, timed

Four marked MULTI-HOP combine frameworks from earlier lessons. One marked BEST ANSWER has two defensible options — pick the better. Budget: 11 minutes for 11 questions — the exam's pace. Stopwatch: 0:00

QUESTION 1 MULTI-HOP

Laptops that processed secret-level documents (Lesson 0019's government ladder) are being retired. The drives will leave organizational custody entirely. Which rung of the ladder, and why that one?

Destroy — classified means physical. The ladder's rule: classification decides the rung, destination confirms it. Secret-level data leaving custody goes to the shredder or incinerator — clear and purge both carry an "effectively" the government does not accept for classified media. For unclassified-sensitive data leaving custody, purge would be the answer.

QUESTION 2

A decommissioned hard disk is passed through a degausser. What is true of the disk afterward?

Dead — servo data erased too. The degausser's field wipes data, servo tracks, and firmware together: purged by execution, unusable for anything afterward. It works only on magnetic media — hard disks and tape — and does nothing to SSDs.

QUESTION 3

A fleet of laptops has used full-device encryption since day one. Decommissioning needs the fastest method that leaves no recoverable data. Which is it?

Crypto-erase the drives. Destroy the key and every sector becomes noise at once — instant, because the encryption was already in place (Lesson 0021's full-device encryption meeting its destiny). Overwriting is slower and misses wear-leveled SSD sectors; formatting removes pointers, not data.

QUESTION 4 MULTI-HOP

An auditor asks for the complete list of systems holding customer personal data. The team cannot produce one — five departments bought their own storage, none recorded. Which failed practice is the root cause, in Lesson 0008's language?

Missing asset inventory. You cannot protect what you cannot name — and undocumented purchases are shadow IT (Lesson 0008) wearing hardware. The fix is inventory plus tracking: every asset owned, tagged, classified, and lifecycle-tracked from procurement to disposal.

QUESTION 5

Before buying a security appliance, the team investigates the vendor: breach history, patch cadence, how long support will continue, and whether the supply chain is tamper-evident. Which practice is this?

Vendor evaluation in procurement. Security decided before the purchase: posture, supply-chain integrity (Lesson 0012's poisoned updates enter here), and support lifetime — buying near end-of-life is buying future EOL. The shredder receipt and the data-retention rule belong to other parts of the lifecycle.

QUESTION 6 BEST ANSWER

Retired finance-server drives, unclassified but sensitive. Two proposals: purge them and donate the machines to a school, or clear them and reuse them as internal test boxes. Both are defensible — which is the better pairing of method to destination?

Purge, then donate to the school. The destination rule: media leaving custody with sensitive data needs purge or better — clear's "recoverable in a laboratory" is an in-org courtesy, not an external guarantee. The defensible near miss: purging for internal reuse is safe but spends effort the destination does not require. The wrong-but-tempting option: clearing before donation leaves data a motivated party could recover.

QUESTION 7 MULTI-HOP

A disposal vendor shreds the drives and returns a signed, dated record itemizing every serial number destroyed. In Lesson 0001's categories and types, what is that certificate?

Managerial and detective. The certificate is proof, not protection — a document that records what happened, discoverable in a later audit. Managerial (documents and process) with detective's job (discover and record). The shredding itself was physical; the paper trail is what the exam is pointing at.

QUESTION 8 MULTI-HOP

Lesson 0019's retention policy expires for a batch of records: paper reports plus solid-state drives. Which disposal pairing fits both media?

Shred the paper, destroy the SSDs. Retention end means secure destruction (Lesson 0019). Paper shreds or incinerates; SSDs need physical destruction because wear-leveling hides sectors from overwriters and degaussers do nothing without a magnetic surface. Expired-and-expiring data gets the highest rung its media allows.
