The CIA Triad and AAA

One win today: given any scenario, name which security goal is under attack, and which access stage a control serves. These two frameworks organize a quarter of the exam's vocabulary.

Why this matters to your mission

Every DoD 8140 work role — cyber defense analyst, incident responder, security control assessor — reports impact the same way: which of the three goals was harmed? And every federal system audit asks the AAA question: who touched what, and were they allowed? These two frameworks are the grammar of the job.

Warm-up — recall Lesson 0001 from memory

Retrieval first, review second. Answer from memory; the feedback below each question is the review.

WARM-UP 1

A leased line is too expensive, so the team uses an encrypted VPN. Which type?

Compensating. The ideal control was impossible, so a substitute was chosen. (Lesson 0001's classic VPN example.)

WARM-UP 2

Each week an analyst reviews firewall logs by hand. Which category and type?

Operational; detective. A person runs a process that looks for what already happened. Managerial is the usual trap — a document did not do this; a person did.

WARM-UP 3

A guard dog patrols the yard at night. Which category and type?

Physical; deterrent. Touchable, and its job is discouragement rather than restraint.

Part 1 — The CIA triad: what security protects

Security has three goals. Every attack harms at least one; every control defends at least one. The triad is confidentiality, integrity, availability — CIA.

GoalPlain meaningAttack examplesControls that defend it
ConfidentialityOnly approved people can read the data.Stolen customer list; snooping traffic; shoulder surfingEncryption; access controls; data classification
IntegrityData stays correct and complete, with nothing added or altered.Altered bank transfer amount; tampered config fileHashing; digital signatures; change management
AvailabilitySystems and data are ready when needed.Denial-of-service (DoS) flood; ransomware lockout; power failureRedundancy; backups; UPS and generator

Exam questions name an attack and ask for the harmed goal. Ransomware is the famous double-hit: files are altered (integrity) and become unreachable (availability) — the exam usually scores it as an availability attack because access is lost.

The fourth goal: non-repudiation

Non-repudiation means proof of an action so strong that the actor cannot deny it. A digital signature on a document is the standard example: only the holder of that private key could have signed. Non-repudiation gets its full treatment with cryptography in Lesson 0004; today you only need the word.

Part 2 — AAA: how access is governed

AAA is three stages every access passes through, in order: authentication, authorization, accounting.

StageQuestion it answersPlain examples
AuthenticationWho are you? Prove it.Password plus one-time code (MFA); badge swipe; fingerprint
AuthorizationWhat may this identity do?Role-based permissions; group membership; IAM policy
AccountingWhat did it actually do?Login events in a central log; session recording; audit trail

The exam trap is ordering: authentication always precedes authorization — permissions are meaningless until identity is proven. Accounting is the stage auditors live in; if a scenario mentions logs or records of actions, the answer is accounting.

Practice — 8 questions, instant feedback

QUESTION 1

An attacker publishes a stolen customer list. Which goal is harmed?

Confidentiality. People who should never read the data can now read it.

QUESTION 2

A flood of junk traffic takes the public site offline. Which goal is harmed?

Availability. Legitimate users cannot reach the service when they need it.

QUESTION 3

An attacker changes a bank transfer amount in transit. Which goal is harmed?

Integrity. The data arrived, but it was no longer correct.

QUESTION 4

A contract's digital signature prevents the signer from later denying they signed. Which concept?

Non-repudiation. The signature is proof the actor cannot deny.

QUESTION 5

At login, a user enters a password and a phone code. Which stage of AAA?

Authentication. Identity is being proven with two kinds of evidence — that is MFA.

QUESTION 6

A finance role permits its members to open the payroll folder. Which stage of AAA?

Authorization. Identity is settled; the question is what the identity may do.

QUESTION 7

Every administrative action is written to a central log for review. Which stage of AAA?

Accounting. Records of what identities did — the auditors' stage.

QUESTION 8

Which goal do daily tested backups defend best?

Availability. Backups (a technical corrective control, Lesson 0001) exist so a system can be ready again after damage.
